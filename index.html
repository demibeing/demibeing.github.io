Project Overview

What makes this bicopter different

Most UAV projects rely on standard PID controllers. This project implements an LQR-I (Linear Quadratic Regulator with Integral) controller on a bicopter — a significantly harder platform due to its strong pitch-yaw coupling and only two degrees of thrust freedom.

The controller runs at 250 Hz on Core 1 of the ESP32 while Core 0 handles WebSocket telemetry and calibration, enabling real-time gain tuning from a browser — no serial cable needed.

Three root-cause fixes were discovered during development and addressed across firmware revisions: pitch-yaw coupling feedforward (K_PY), throttle-normalised roll/yaw outputs, and a throttle-aware complementary filter accelerometer weight.

All outputs now route through a PCA9685 I²C PWM driver, freeing all ESP32 GPIO timer channels and enabling clean I²C bus sharing with the MPU6050.

Hardware Stack

Components & design decisions

⚡

ESP32

Dual-core 240 MHz. Core 1 locked to flight control at configMAX_PRIORITIES−1. FreeRTOS mutex protects shared I²C bus.

📐

MPU6050 IMU

6-DOF at 0x68 on the same I²C bus. Gyro + accel fused via throttle-aware complementary filter to estimate roll, pitch, and yaw rate.

🔌

PCA9685

16-ch I²C PWM at 0x40, 50 Hz. Drives 2× MG995 servos and 2× 30A ESCs. Replaces direct GPIO timers, eliminating PWM contention.

🔧

MG995 Servos

One per arm, tilt-rotor mechanism. Roll is servo differential. Yaw is servo common-mode. ±300 µs servo limit from neutral.

⚙️

EMAX 935KV BLDC

860 g thrust each at full 3S throttle. Counter-rotating pair (CW + CCW) cancels torque reaction in hover.

🔋

3S 2500 mAh LiPo

Mounted centrally below the frame to keep CG under the motor plane, improving pendulum stability margin.

Control Architecture

LQR-I Sequence & Flow

Step 01
RC snapshot & failsafe
ISR-driven PWM capture on five channels. A failsafe fires if signal age exceeds 100 ms, throttle is out of range, or AUX channel is high — cutting motors and centering servos immediately.
Step 02
IMU read & complementary filter
MPU6050 provides gyro (p, q, r) and accel. Filter blends gyro integration with accel-derived angle. Weight α = 0.02 × (1 − 0.75 × thr_norm) — reducing accelerometer trust at high throttle to reject vibration.
Step 03
LQR-I control law
u_roll = K_PHI·φ_err − K_P·p + K_I_R·∫φ_err  |  u_pitch = K_THETA·θ_err − K_Q·q + K_I_P·∫θ_err  |  u_yaw = K_R·r_err − K_RD·ṙ + K_I_Y·∫ψ_err + K_PY·u_pitch
Step 04
Throttle normalisation
u_roll and u_yaw are divided by thr_norm = clamp((thr−1000)/1000, 0.3, 1.0) so the effective gain stays constant as throttle varies — preventing sluggish response at hover and over-correction at full power.
Step 05
Output mixer → PCA9685
f_srv = MID + u_roll_n + u_yaw_n  |  b_srv = MID − u_roll_n + u_yaw_n  |  esc_f = base − u_pitch  |  esc_b = base + u_pitch. Four I²C writes per 4 ms cycle.

Test Results

PID → LQR-I development milestones

⚠ Test 1 — Issue found

Pendulum oscillation at liftoff

Side-to-side oscillatory motion appeared immediately after liftoff. Instability grew with throttle. Root causes identified: pitch-yaw coupling without feedforward, and throttle-independent normalisation of roll/yaw outputs.

Root causes fixed in v3 firmware

Added K_PY feedforward · throttle normalisation · throttle-aware acc weight

✓ Test 2 — Pitch stable

Cascaded pitch stability achieved

Pitch axis stabilised using a cascaded inner-outer loop. Inner loop controls angular velocity at high frequency; outer loop controls absolute angle. Stable gains: Outer P = 2.5, Inner P = 0.005, I = 0.001, D = 0.021.

Upgraded to LQR-I in v4

PCA9685 integration · EEPROM gain persistence · WebSocket live tuning

✓ Infrastructure

Custom ground control GUI

WebSocket telemetry at 20 Hz streams roll, pitch, target setpoints, all 10 LQR-I gains, and RC channel values. Custom Processing IDE GUI shows real-time attitude plots, servo feedback bars, and artificial horizon.

→ Upcoming

Hover test & full flight validation

Analyse PID tuning issues identified. Conduct untethered hover test. Refine LQR-I gains for stable hover. Long-term: validate energy efficiency and evaluate for real-world UAV applications.
